## 1.1 Systems of Linear Equations

A linear equation in the variables x₁, ..., xₙ is an equation that can be written in the form a₁x₁ + a₂x₂ + ... + aₙxₙ = b, where b and the coefficients a₁, ..., aₙ are real or complex numbers, usually known in advance.

The subscript n may be any positive integer. In textbook examples, n is normally between 2 and 4, or even larger in real-life problems.

The equations 4x₁ + 5x₂ = x₁ + x₂ + 2√6 and 3x₁ + 5x₂ + 2x₁x₂ + 2√6 are not linear because of the term 2x₁x₂ in the second equation and √x₁ in the second.

are not linear because of the presence of x₁x₂ in the first equation and √(x₁ + 6) in the second.
